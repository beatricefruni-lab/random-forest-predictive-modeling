Empirical Comparison with CART & Applied Consumer Analytics in R | Beatrice Fruni

1. Executive Summary & Research Background

Developed as part of my Bachelor's thesis at the University of Trento (supervised by Prof. Emanuele Taufer), this project assesses the mathematical architecture, variance reduction, and predictive accuracy of Leo Breiman's Random Forest algorithm compared against single Classification and Regression Trees (CART).

While individual decision trees provide high interpretability, they are susceptible to high variance and overfitting. Random Forests resolve these limitations by introducing a dual-randomization mechanism:

  • Bootstrap Aggregation (Bagging): Training multiple decision trees on random subsets with replacement.
  • Random Subspace Method: Selecting a subset of features \(m \approx \sqrt{p}\) at each split to decorrelate individual trees.

3. Hyperparameter Tuning (\(mtry\) Search)

An iterative experiment was conducted across all potential predictor counts (\(mtry \in [1, 13]\)) to track Out-Of-Bag (OOB) error convergence alongside test set errors. 

# Excerpt: Iterative Tuning Loop in R
oob_err  <- double(13)
test_err <- double(13)

for (m in 1:13) {
  rf <- randomForest(medv ~ ., data = bh_train, mtry = m, ntree = 400)
  oob_err[m]  <- rf$mse[400]
  pred        <- predict(rf, bh_test)
  test_err[m] <- mean((bh_test$medv - pred)^2)
}

Findings: The Out-of-Bag error stabilized at \(mtry = 5\) (error = 9.40), while test error reached its lowest boundary at \(mtry = 6\) (error = 15.60). Increasing variables beyond this threshold elevated correlation among individual trees without adding predictive information.

5. Commercial Application: Predictive Analytics in Digital Marketing

The project translates algorithmic ensembling into digital marketing, evaluating a high-dimensional consumer behavior dataset (50,000 observations):

  • Behavioral Telemetry: Shopping cart addition frequency, browsing session recency, and promotion sensitivity.
  • Objective: Predicting 7-day conversion and purchase volume.
  • Outcome: The Random Forest classifier demonstrated superior precision over classical logistic regression benchmarks, successfully isolating 21,637 active buyers and proving its efficacy for customer segment targeting and ad spend optimization.
